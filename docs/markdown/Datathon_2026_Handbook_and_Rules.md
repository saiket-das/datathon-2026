### 1. Overview

Datathon 2026 is an annual data analytics competition organised by Universiti Malaya Data Analytics Club (UMDAC) to introduce challenges from real-world business problems and their respective analytical way to extract business insights from data. Unlike conventional programming competitions, this event rewards meaningful insights and practical business value, not technical complexity alone. The Objective of Datathon 2026 are:

1. Encourage students to solve real-world business challenges with the application of data analytics & data science knowledge through collaboration, communication & teamwork

2. Train participants' analytical thinking, critical thinking & problem-solving skills

3. Promote innovation by encouraging participants to generate practical, data-driven business recommendations.

### 4. Bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge & industry expectations

Participants are expected to :

- Analyse the dataset & Understand the problem domain

- Communicate findings & Propose practical recommendations. Datathon 2026 will run throughout November 2026 in a hybrid format, containing Online Workshops & Preliminary Round and a Physical Final Round at the Faculty of Computer Science and Information Technology (FSKTM), University of Malaya. Throughout the competition, participants will

- Attend workshops by experienced speakers

### Rules and Regulations

### 1. Online Meeting Etiquette

1.1 Participants are expected to be punctual for all scheduled online sessions.

1.2 Participants must refrain from any behaviour that disrupts the flow of the event or interferes with other participants.

1.3 Participants must join online sessions using their registered names for easy identification.

1.4 The moderator reserves the right to remove any participant who disrupts the session or fails to comply with these rules or the moderator's reasonable instructions.

### 2. Datasets

2.1 Competition datasets constitute the intellectual property of the curator (organizer / sponsor) and are distributed to participants for the sole purpose of the competition.

2.2 Redistribution, publication or unauthorised disclosure of competition datasets, in whole or in part, to any third party is strictly prohibited.

2.3 Participants must use only the datasets provided by the organiser for their primary analysis. External datasets must not be used as additional primary data sources to ensure a level playing field among participants.

2.4 However, external reference statistics from credible sources (e.g., research papers, government reports, industry publications) are allowed for participants to calibrate, justify and scale their analysis.

2.5 Participants must properly acknowledge all external references using the APA format. External URLs to any web-searched content must be embedded in the citation.

### 3. Academic Integrity

3.1 Participants are expected to uphold the highest degree of integrity throughout the competition.

3.2 All submissions by participating teams must be original and free from plagiarism. All external sources must be properly cited.

3.3 Participants must not share their solutions, source code, analyses or other substantive competition work with participants outside their registered teams.

3.4 Participating teams must provide sufficient documentation of their methodologies, code and solutions, where applicable, through code comments, supporting documentation or presentation slides to facilitate evaluation by the judges.

3.5 Participants may use any appropriate software, programming languages, analytical tools or other technologies to develop their solutions, provided that their use complies with these Rules and Regulations.

3.6 Participants are permitted to use AI tools, including LLMs to assist in developing their solutions. No separate declaration of AI usage is required. However, participants remain responsible for their submissions and must be able to explain and justify their methodologies, analyses and solutions.

3.7 Participants may direct competition-related questions to their assigned mentor during a scheduled Q&A session.

3.8 The organiser reserves the right to investigate suspected academic misconduct, including cheating, plagiarism and unauthorised collaboration. Participants found to have committed academic misconduct may be subject to penalties, including disqualification, depending on the nature and severity of the violation.

### 4. Submission

4.1 The preliminary round of the competition will run for 144 hours (6 days), commencing on 13 November 2026 at 11:50 AM and ending on 19 November 2026 at 12:00 PM. during which participants are required to identify relevant problem statements, analyse the provided datasets, develop their solutions and prepare their submissions.

4.2 For the preliminary round, participating teams must submit their presentation slides and dashboard visualisations through the designated submission link, in accordance with the prescribed submission requirements.

4.3 Dashboard visualisations and presentation slides must be submitted in PDF format. Presentation slides must clearly present the team's methodology, key findings, insights and proposed solutions. No pitch video is required for the preliminary round.

4.4 Participants are expected to produce approximately 5 to 15 dashboards in total, based on one dashboard per feature-type group identified in the dataset. Presentation slides should comprise approximately 10 to 20 slides, including the introduction and methodology, with a maximum of one slide summarising the business rules and insights for each feature-type group.

4.5 Participants must ensure that all submission files are complete, accessible and in the prescribed formats before the submission deadline.

4.6 The preliminary-round submission link will close on 19 November 2026 at 12:00 PM. No further submissions or amendments will be accepted after the deadline.

### 5. General Rules

5.1 This competition is open to all university students who are interested in applying data analytics to real-world problems.

5.2 Participants may register individually or in teams of up to 3 members.

5.3 Working solutions are evaluated based on their respective merits, taking into account criteria such as methodology, accuracy, creativity, quality of insights, practical relevance and overall impact, in accordance with the official judging rubric.

5.4 In evaluating reconstructed business rules and statistical models, judges will consider how accurately the proposed solutions reflect the underlying patterns, distributions and relationships in the datasets. Alternative modelling approaches may be accepted where they produce materially comparable results, and exact replication of the original methods is not required.

5.5 Participants are encouraged to dress appropriately and professionally for all official competition events, particularly the physical Final Round.

5.6 Participants who violate the rules and regulations above may be subject to appropriate penalties, including disqualification, depending on the nature and severity of the violation.
